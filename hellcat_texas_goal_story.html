The Road to the Hellcat & Texas

A personal roadmap from $1,000/month → Hellcat → Texas
THE ROAD TO THE HELLCAT & TEXAS

A Story About Starting With $1,000 a Month and Building a Bigger Life

CHAPTER 1 — WHERE I STARTED

I didn't start with thousands of dollars in the bank.

I started with about $1,000 a month.

I had big goals, but I also had bills. My phone was $60. Wi-Fi was $60. Food was about $50. That was $170 before I even started thinking about the future.

But I made a decision: I wasn't going to let where I started decide where I finished.

My first job was not to become rich overnight. My first job was to become consistent.

October 2026 became my setup month.

I would cover my essentials, build a small cushion, save about $500 if I could, and get everything ready for DoorDash. I would get my work setup together, keep track of my hours and earnings, and prepare for November 2.

The goal wasn't perfection.

The goal was to start.

CHAPTER 2 — NOVEMBER 2

November 2, 2026 was different.

That was the day I started treating my goals like a real plan.

I wasn't just saying, "I want a Hellcat."

I had a date.

JULY 28, 2027.

I wasn't just saying, "I want to move to Texas."

I had another date.

JULY 28, 2028.

And I had a picture in my head of what I was working toward: a Hellcat that I owned outright, then a beautiful Texas home with space, a pool, and a future I had built myself.

My DoorDash target became $240 a day.

At $240 a day for 30 days, that would be $7,200 of gross earnings in a 30-day month.

But I learned something important: gross earnings are not the same as money I can spend. Taxes, food, transportation, insurance, equipment, and other costs matter.

So I stopped looking only at the big number.

I started tracking what I actually kept.

CHAPTER 3 — THE DAILY MISSION

Every morning, I had one job:

SHOW UP.

I didn't have to think about the entire $60,000.

I just had to win the day.

If my target was $240, I could break the day into smaller checkpoints.

Morning.
Midday.
Afternoon.
Evening.

Every completed order moved the number.

$20.
$40.
$60.
$100.
$150.
$200.
$220.
$240.

And when the day was slow, I didn't panic.

I checked my actual hourly rate.

If I was averaging $15 an hour, $240 would take about 16 hours of earning time. That taught me something: I needed to improve my earnings per hour, not simply work endlessly.

Once I had a car and could access better-paying orders, I would track dollars per active hour and number of orders instead of assuming every $20–$40 order would appear on demand.

The mission was to work smarter as well as harder.

CHAPTER 4 — THE MONEY RULE

I knew something about myself:

Money sitting on my spending card was easier for me to spend.

So I changed the system.

The Hellcat money would not live in my everyday spending account.

Bills money had a place.

Spending money had a place.

Savings had a separate place.

Emergency money had a place.

I wasn't going to depend on motivation every time I saw a balance.

I was going to make the money harder to touch.

Every dollar had a job.

CHAPTER 5 — THE HELLCAT FUND

The number on the screen kept growing.

$10,000.

Then $20,000.

Then $30,000.

Then $40,000.

Then $50,000.

The goal was $60,000.

I imagined the day I would walk into a dealership knowing I wasn't asking someone to finance my dream.

I was paying cash.

But I also remembered the rule:

I would not spend my entire savings balance just because I reached the purchase price.

The Hellcat needed insurance.
Registration.
Gas.
Maintenance.
Tires.
Unexpected repairs.

So I would buy the car only when the purchase money and a separate cushion were both ready.

Then came the date I had written down:

JULY 28, 2027.

THE HELLCAT.

PAID FOR.

OWNED BY ME.

That moment wasn't the end.

It was the next chapter.

CHAPTER 6 — THE DREAM GETS BIGGER

After the Hellcat, I didn't stop.

I changed the goal.

Now I was building for Texas.

The house I had been watching was 9110 Rhapsody Ln in Houston, Texas, or a similar property if that listing changed before I was ready.

The plan wasn't to save the entire house price in cash unless my income eventually made that realistic.

The practical target was to build a strong house fund for the down payment, closing costs, and emergency reserves.

I set my long-term income goal around $7,000 a month.

That is roughly $233 a day if spread across 30 days.

If I could save $3,000 a month toward the house, 22 months would be about $66,000.

At $3,500 a month, it would be about $77,000.

That became the next mission.

CHAPTER 7 — THE TWO JULY DATES

I kept two dates in my head.

JULY 28, 2027 — HELLCAT.

JULY 28, 2028 — TEXAS.

The first date represented discipline.

The second represented freedom.

But I learned not to obsess over the calendar.

If the car took longer, I would adjust.

If DoorDash was slower, I would adjust.

If my income increased, I would adjust.

The dates were targets, not magic.

What mattered was that every month I was closer than the month before.

CHAPTER 8 — THE MOVE

Eventually, the day came when I was no longer looking at Texas from far away.

I had money saved.

I had income.

I had transportation.

I had a plan.

I checked the current house price.

I checked the mortgage requirements.

I checked insurance, taxes, closing costs, and the inspection.

I didn't buy a house just because I was excited.

I bought it because the numbers worked.

Then I packed.

The same person who once looked at a $1,000 monthly income was now driving toward a new life.

DC was behind me.

Texas was ahead.

And I knew the biggest part of the journey wasn't the car or the house.

It was the person I had become while working toward them.

THE DAILY PROMISE

I don't need to become successful in one day.

I need to make one good decision today.

Work when it's time to work.

Save when it's time to save.

Pay my bills.

Protect my money.

Track my numbers.

Take care of my body.

Don't spend tomorrow's money today.

Don't let one bad day become one bad week.

Don't let one slow week become the end of the dream.

The Hellcat is a milestone.

Texas is a milestone.

The real goal is building a life where I control my money instead of my money controlling me.
